November 6, 1942: the fighting on Madagascar ended. It’s one of the least-known campaigns of the war, fought to keep the world’s fourth-largest island out of Japanese hands.

In early 1942, Japan’s navy was rampaging across the Indian Ocean, and Madagascar was ruled by Vichy France, which had already let Japanese forces into French Indochina. Britain feared Japanese submarines would soon be operating from the island’s harbors, astride the convoy routes to Egypt and India.

On May 5, 1942, British forces stormed ashore near the great naval base of Diego Suarez. Weeks later, Japanese midget submarines slipped into its harbor and torpedoed the battleship HMS Ramillies.

Vichy Governor-General Armand Annet refused to surrender the rest of the island. British, South African and East African troops, including the King’s African Rifles, pushed hundreds of miles south through the highlands until an armistice was granted on November 6.
