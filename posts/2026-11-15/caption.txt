November 15, 1942: in the dark hours after midnight off Guadalcanal, USS Washington wrecked the Japanese battleship Kirishima in one of only two battleship-versus-battleship fights of the Pacific War.

It was the climax of the three-day Naval Battle of Guadalcanal. Japan was making an all-out push to retake the island, sending battleships to shell Henderson Field and a convoy of transports packed with troops and supplies.

Admiral Willis Lee’s battleships Washington and South Dakota met the Japanese force near Savo Island. South Dakota suffered an electrical failure and took a pounding, but Washington, unseen in the darkness, opened fire at close range with her radar-directed guns. Within minutes Kirishima was burning and flooding. She capsized and sank by 3:25 a.m.

At dawn, the last four Japanese transports ran themselves aground on Guadalcanal, where American planes and artillery set them ablaze. Japan never again sent battleships against Henderson Field, and the campaign for Guadalcanal swung decisively to the Allies.
