November 14, 1940: for ten hours, 515 German bombers pounded the English city of Coventry. By morning, its medieval heart was gone.

The Luftwaffe code-named the raid Moonlight Sonata. Its targets were Coventry’s factories, which built aircraft engines, vehicles and munitions, but wave after wave of high explosives and incendiaries set the whole city center ablaze.

An estimated 568 people were killed, and more than 4,300 homes were destroyed. The medieval cathedral of St Michael burned to a shell. Coventry’s anti-aircraft guns fired more than 6,700 rounds that night and brought down just one bomber.

Joseph Goebbels began using a new word for wiping out a city: “coventriert,” or “Coventried.” Coventry chose a different legacy. Its ruined cathedral became a symbol of peace and reconciliation, and after the war the city twinned with Dresden.
