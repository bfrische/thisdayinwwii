November 11, 1940: twenty-one fabric-covered biplanes took off from HMS Illustrious and, in a single night, knocked out half of Italy’s battleships.

The Fairey Swordfish was nicknamed the “Stringbag” and looked like a leftover from the First World War. But its crews flew into Taranto, home of the Italian battle fleet, at night, past barrage balloons and through heavy anti-aircraft fire, and dropped torpedoes into the shallow harbour.

Torpedoes tore into the battleships Littorio, Conte di Cavour and Caio Duilio. Only two Swordfish were shot down. Littorio was back in service in about four months, but Conte di Cavour was never fully repaired.

It was the first all-aircraft ship-to-ship naval attack in history, and Japanese naval planners studied it closely. Thirteen months later, they struck Pearl Harbor with a far larger version of the same idea.

Did you know Taranto was a model for Pearl Harbor? 👇
