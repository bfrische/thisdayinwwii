November 8, 1942: Operation Torch. More than 70,000 American and British troops landed in French North Africa, America’s first big step into the ground war against Hitler.

Commanded by a then little-known general named Dwight D. Eisenhower, three task forces landed around Casablanca, Oran and Algiers. One of them had sailed directly from the United States, crossing the Atlantic through waters patrolled by U-boats.

The defenders weren’t Germans but Vichy French troops, and no one knew whether they would fight. American soldiers were ordered not to shoot unless fired upon, and some landing parties came ashore behind huge American flags, hoping the French would hold their fire. Many didn’t. Around Casablanca and Oran the fighting lasted days, while in Algiers a French Resistance uprising helped the city fall on the first day.

Within days the French in North Africa switched sides. Torch helped trap the Axis armies in Africa between the Allies in the west and Montgomery’s Eighth Army in the east, and gave American soldiers their first hard lessons in fighting the Germans.
