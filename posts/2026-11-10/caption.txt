November 10, 1942: “Now this is not the end. It is not even the beginning of the end. But it is, perhaps, the end of the beginning.” — Winston Churchill

Churchill spoke those words at the Lord Mayor’s Luncheon at Mansion House in London. For three years Britain had absorbed one defeat after another: Dunkirk, Greece, Crete, Singapore, Tobruk. Now, finally, he had victories to talk about.

In Egypt, Montgomery’s Eighth Army had just routed Rommel at El Alamein. Two days earlier, American and British troops had landed in Morocco and Algeria. Soon after, church bells, silenced since 1940 and reserved to warn of a German invasion, rang out across Britain in celebration.

Churchill was careful not to promise too much. Two and a half more years of war lay ahead. But the tide had begun to turn, and the British people could hear it in his words.
