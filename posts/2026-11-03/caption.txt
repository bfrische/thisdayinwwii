November 3, 1944: Japan released the first of about 9,300 balloon bombs aimed at North America, the first weapon in history with intercontinental range.

They were called Fu-Go: hydrogen balloons made of paper, launched from the coast of Honshu. Carried by the winter jet stream, they drifted some 5,000 miles across the Pacific in about three days, then dropped incendiary and anti-personnel bombs meant to start forest fires and spread fear.

About 300 were found or spotted in the United States, Canada and Mexico. U.S. officials asked newspapers and radio stations to say nothing, so Japan would never learn whether its weapon was working. Hearing nothing, Japan halted the launches in April 1945.

But the silence had a cost. On May 5, 1945, a pregnant woman and five children on a church picnic near Bly, Oregon, found a balloon that had come down in the woods. It exploded, killing all six. They were the only people killed by enemy action in the continental United States during World War II.
