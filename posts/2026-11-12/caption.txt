November 12, 1944: RAF Lancasters finally sank “the Beast.” Hit by at least two 12,000-pound Tallboy bombs, the battleship Tirpitz rolled over within minutes.

Tirpitz, sister ship of the Bismarck, spent most of the war hiding in Norwegian fjords and never fought an enemy warship. Yet her mere presence tied down Allied battleships for years. In July 1942, fear that she was about to strike made convoy PQ 17 scatter, and German planes and U-boats sank most of its merchant ships.

The Allies came after her with midget submarines, carrier aircraft and heavy bombers. On November 12, Lancasters of No. 9 Squadron and No. 617, the famous “Dambusters,” found her anchored off Tromsø.

Between 940 and 1,204 of her crew were killed. The British didn’t lose a single man.
