November 4, 1942: Rommel’s army broke at El Alamein. Defying Hitler’s order to stand fast, the “Desert Fox” ordered a retreat that wouldn’t stop until his forces reached Tunisia.

For nearly two weeks, Montgomery’s Eighth Army had slugged its way through minefields and gun lines in the Egyptian desert. Hitler’s instructions to Rommel were blunt: show your troops “no other road than that to victory or death.”

By the afternoon of November 4, British armor had broken through, the commander of the Afrika Korps, General Wilhelm Ritter von Thoma, had been captured, and Italian divisions were surrounded. At 5:30 p.m., unable to wait any longer for Hitler’s reply, Rommel ordered the retreat.

Egypt and the Suez Canal were safe, and the desert war had turned for good. Churchill later wrote: “Before Alamein we never had a victory. After Alamein we never had a defeat.”
