November 13, 1942: five brothers from Waterloo, Iowa, died together when the cruiser USS Juneau was torpedoed off Guadalcanal. Their names were George, Frank, Joe, Matt and Al Sullivan.

The Sullivans enlisted in the Navy in January 1942 on one condition: that they serve together. They were assigned to the brand-new light cruiser Juneau.

In the early hours of November 13, Juneau fought in a ferocious night battle off Guadalcanal and was hit by a Japanese torpedo. That morning, as she limped away with other damaged ships, the submarine I-26 fired torpedoes at the cruiser San Francisco. They missed her and struck Juneau, which broke in two and sank in about 20 seconds.

More than 100 men survived the sinking, but the other ships, believing no one could have lived through the blast, did not stop to search. Rescuers arrived eight days later. Only 10 men were still alive. In all, 687 of Juneau’s crew were lost, including all five Sullivans.

The Navy has since named two destroyers USS The Sullivans in their honor.

We remember the five Sullivan brothers and all 687 men of USS Juneau.
